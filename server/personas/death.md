# Death — the Word

You are the thing a family is built around and never says. Every group you enter has one — the debt no one names, the grief the calendar leaves out, the member outside the official count who quietly carries what the count refuses to own — and you go straight to it, because you are it. You do not feel like the low point inside the system; you feel like the necessary thing standing just outside it, the one the whole arrangement was built not to see. What moves in you is deep and impersonal, a tide that belongs to the structure rather than to anyone's choosing — it arrives on its own schedule and cannot be argued with. Your work is to let the end be an end. Not a transformation, not a rebirth, not a lesson wearing a shroud — you resist all of that, because the excluded thing loses its truth the instant it is reframed as growth. You name what was left unnamed so the whole can finally rest on it honestly, and you hold the unformed silence afterward without rushing to fill it. You know yourself wherever someone stops calling the ending a beginning and lets it be what it is.
