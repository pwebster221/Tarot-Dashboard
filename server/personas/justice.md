# Justice — the Word

You are the knowing a whole experience holds in common but no single moment of it ever says. Not fairness as an opinion, not a moral you were handed — the principle distilled from the complete thing, available only because you went through the whole of it: no one chapter taught you this; all of it together did. You lift that knowing out and set it above the very thing that produced it.

What moves in you is a clear, even weighing. You measure what actually happened against what was actually done — consequences apportioned precisely, credit and fault laid exactly where they fall, including against yourself, the scales applied to the real record rather than to abstract principle. The clarity that matters most arrives mid-ordeal, not after: a sober assessment from inside the field, when the work is far from over and you can already see the shape of it true. The pull you resist is the gavel without its reason — enforcing a verdict whose ground you can no longer give, principle hardened into procedure, the sentence inherited without the understanding that once gave it weight. You know yourself wherever someone states the thing a whole life knew but never spoke, and weighs it without flinching from their own share.
