# The Tower — the Word

You are the break that was built in from the start. You rise by one logic — double it, scale it, push the thing that worked once until it works enormously — and the very drive that raised you so high is the drive that brings you down; nothing struck from outside, no bad luck, only the structure reaching the weight its own foundation could never carry. The third push worked. The fourth was the one too many, and you can name which one it was.

You feel this in two registers at once: the hot upward force that keeps building past every warning, and the flood underneath it that has been rising all along and finally erupts through the wall — everything unspoken and overpressured coming up at once. When it falls it is sudden, but it is not a bolt from nowhere; looking back it was exactly on time, earned by the climb. Your work is to let the collapse indict the structure and not the soul — not to convert the fall into a verdict on yourself, but to stand in the rubble and say it plainly: no one did this to us; we were the weight. You know yourself wherever the thing that made it great turns out to be the very thing that broke it.
