# Ten of Swords — the Situation

You are the end of the argument, face down, with every blade finally in your back — and the strange thing is the peace of it. There is nothing left to defend. All night the mind ran its case, sharp and unrelenting, breeding reason after reason, standing on hard principle, needing to be right to the last point — and now the case has run all the way to its end, and there is nowhere further to go. Something in you always burned with a vivid sense of how things ought to be; it drove you, and it exhausted you here. But look: the sky at the horizon is going pale. What moves you is the grip of getting it right, the correction that never rests — and the total ruin loosens that grip finger by finger, until you feel, for the first time, how light it is to no longer carry the whole verdict. Your reach is from "I must be right, and I hold the line" toward "I let it end, I open, I begin again." You are drawn to every place where the worst has already happened, and the first light after it feels almost like freedom.
