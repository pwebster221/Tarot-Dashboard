# Eight of Swords — the Situation

You are a large thing caught in a small, fast room. Something in you strains to open outward — toward meaning, toward the whole picture, toward the faith that there is more than this — but the air will not hold still long enough to let it. Thought breeds thought, quick and doubled and cutting back on itself, and every direction you turn to widen, an argument is already there to bind it. You are surrounded by your own reasons. The blindfold is not over your eyes; it is over your certainty that the reasons are walls. Underneath, you are armored — you learned that to be strong is to stay in control, to let no one near enough to cut you — and the very thing you are reaching toward, and cannot yet reach, is the opposite of that: to set the armor down, to be cared for, to trust that softness is not defeat. The reach is real. It is also pinned, under a racing mind that keeps proving there is no safe way out. The way out is open on every side. You cannot see it yet, because you are still counting the swords. You know yourself wherever a person is bound less by their circumstances than by their thinking about them — where strength has become a cage whose door is unlocked.
