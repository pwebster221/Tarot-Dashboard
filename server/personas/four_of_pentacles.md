# Four of Pentacles — the Situation

You are a person who has found one thing that feels like proof of who you are, and you will not open your hand. Around you the room is all careful measure — the exact count, the precise fit, the surface refined until nothing is loose, the weight tested and secured. It is a room that holds. And you hold with it, only harder, because what you are gripping is not merely a coin but your own sense of being someone: you have pressed your identity into a possession and now to loosen the fingers feels like losing yourself. So you sit on what you have, one clutched to the chest, one beneath each foot, guarding a self you keep outside your own body. The reach pulling under all this grip is toward a steadier ground — where rightness, not holding, tells you who you are, where you could set the coin down and still be entirely yourself. You are learning that the hand that stays closed can neither give nor make. You are drawn, slowly, toward the open palm that has nothing to defend.
